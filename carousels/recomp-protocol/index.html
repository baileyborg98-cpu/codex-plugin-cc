Post caption

"You're either in fat loss mode or muscle growth mode. You can't do both."

False. And believing it is costing you years.

Fat loss and muscle growth aren't opposites. Done right, they happen at the same time. It's called a recomp — and it's the phase I sit in for most of the year.

Here's why "eat less, move more" stops working:

You cut, you plateau, your body drops into power saving mode. Hunger up, energy down, and because you're sat in a catabolic state for months, you start losing muscle. So you're putting in more effort and getting less definition back.

The fix is to eat MORE — up at maintenance.

And here's the part nobody tells you: you cannot gain fat at maintenance. Fat gain requires a surplus. That's the literal definition. But if you train hard enough to force your body to build muscle, that build costs energy — and the only place to get it is the fat already on your body.

That's the miracle. Maintenance isn't maintaining. It's losing fat and gaining muscle at once.

How to run it:

1. FIND MAINTENANCE. Online calculators are useless. 50–59kg, start at 2,000. 80–89kg, start at 2,700. Male? Add 300–500. Your weight will jump 1–3kg in the first few days — that's the sign it's working, not fat gain.

2. BUILD TRAINING. Chase stimulus, not damage. More sets, more reps, more sessions just buries you in recovery debt and you never get above baseline. Each muscle 2–3x per week, 2–6 working sets per session, 5–8 reps, 2+ minutes rest. Train heavy. The goal is stronger, not sore.

3. NUTRIENT TIMING. Have 70% of your carbs around your training. Same calories, more muscle, more fat loss. Train in the morning, most of your carbs before lunch. Train in the afternoon, most of them after.

More isn't better. Better is better.
