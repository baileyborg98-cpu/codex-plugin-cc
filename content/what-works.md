# What Works

Breakdowns of the two best performing fitness reels. Two different engines. They do different jobs and the numbers prove it.

---

# FORMAT A: the accusation reel

**The abs one. 57 seconds. 93.6% non followers. Comment rate 0.6%, above baseline.**

## The script, timestamped

```
00:00  Let me guess, you've been dieting for around eight months
       and you're confused about why you don't have abs
00:05  You don't have abs because you've been dieting for 8 months.
00:08  Hear me out, your body's not stupid.
00:09  You cut the food, you added the cardio, and it did exactly
       what it was built to do.
00:15  It flicked itself into power saving mode.
00:17  And we're going to use your phone as an example.
00:19  When your phone's on 20%, your screen dims, everything shuts
       down just to keep it alive on less energy.
00:26  Now, that's the same with your body.
00:27  Less energy, less movement.
00:29  And now we're burning muscle just to pay for it.
00:31  So you're putting in more than everyone else around you and
       you're getting smaller for it.
00:35  Meanwhile, Jimmy over here, the person you've been comparing
       yourself to is eating 2,700 a day and has a six-pack.
00:43  It has nothing to do with genetics.
00:45  Jimmy is just someone who stopped punishing themselves.
00:47  Jimmy also knows you can't gain fat at maintenance.
00:51  So after this, eat more, train better, and stop being scared
       of a fucking bread rock.
```

## The seven moves

**1. Guess their exact situation, with a number.** Eight months. Not "if you're struggling with fat loss." A specific number makes the viewer feel personally accused. The number is the hook, not the topic.

**2. Payoff is the hook sentence reversed.** Same words, causality flipped. No new information needed to feel the hit.

**3. Buy time out loud.** "Hear me out, your body's not stupid." Four words that tell someone about to swipe that the explanation is coming.

**4. One metaphor, signposted, extended, bridged back.** Announce it, phone at 20%, then "now, that's the same with your body." Never two metaphors.

**5. Name the injustice.** "You're putting in more than everyone else around you and you're getting smaller for it." Recognition, not information. They have felt this and never heard it said.

**6. Give the comparison a name.** Jimmy appears three times and gets funnier each go. Abstract "people with abs" is forgettable. Jimmy is a character, and characters get quoted in comments.

**7. Close on imperative plus one absurd concrete image.** Only swear in the script, landing on the funniest word. Bread rock beats any motivational line.

# FORMAT B: the satire reel

**The cookie dough one. 39 seconds. 96.3% non followers. Comment rate 0.0%, below baseline.**

## The script, timestamped

```
00:00  If you actually want to lose weight, like actually, actually
       want to lose weight, there's only one way to do it.
00:05  And that is simply just chicken, rice, and broccoli prepped
       on Sunday.
00:09  Six containers all lined up in a fridge in a salad every time
       at lunchtime with no dressing because that dressing's got
       calories.
00:16  And you do it for 12 weeks straight.
00:19  And you don't deviate once because the second you eat anything
       else, you've undone the entire week and you're back at the
       start.
00:29  That's what they tell you anyways.
00:31  This fits my numbers and it has done all year round and I've
       never once had a container of broccoli in my fridge.
```

## The four moves

**1. Over commit to the earnest setup.** "Like actually, actually want to lose weight." The repetition builds a tone of total sincerity so the fall is further.

**2. Escalate the absurdity, flatten the delivery.** Six containers, then no dressing because calories, then 12 weeks, then don't deviate once, then you've undone the entire week. Each rung more ridiculous, each one said more plainly. Never wink.

**3. Turn in four words.** "That's what they tell you anyways." Casual, dismissive, does the entire job. No explanation of the joke.

**4. Personal proof, not argument.** "This fits my numbers and I've never once had a container of broccoli in my fridge." You are the evidence. Nothing to debate.

## The craft note that makes it work

The on screen text carries the joke, the audio plays it straight. Text says the claim with a line struck through it plus "actually, not really", so the viewer knows it's satire from second one and can enjoy watching you commit. Meanwhile the cookie dough in your hand is arguing the whole time without a word.

That split is the format. Text reveals, audio deadpans, visual contradicts.

# WHAT THE TWO CURVES SAY TOGETHER

| | Accusation (abs) | Satire (cookie dough) |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 57s | 39s |
| Payoff lands at | 0:05, 9% in | 0:29, 74% in |
| Retention at 5s | ~50% | ~55% |
| Retention at end | ~20% | ~18% |
| Comment rate | 0.6%, higher | 0.0%, lower |
| Non followers | 93.6% | 96.3% |

## 1. The first five seconds cost you half your audience every single time

Both reels lose 45 to 50% before second five, then go nearly flat for the rest. Identical shape on two completely different formats. This is not a content problem, it's a structural one, and it's the highest leverage fix available to you.

The abs reel is the painful example. Its single best line, the reversal, sits at 0:05, so half the audience left before hearing it. Compress the setup and land the payoff by second three. "Let me guess, eight months dieting, still no abs" then straight into the reversal.

## 2. Payoff timing is format dependent, so don't copy one rule across both

Accusation format needs the payoff almost immediately, because the tension is a question the viewer wants answered. Satire can hold its turn until 74% through, because the tension is watching whether you'll break character. The cookie dough in shot is doing the work that the reversal does in Format A.

Never put a satire turn early. Never put an accusation payoff late.

## 3. Satire travels furthest, accusation gets the comments

96.3% versus 93.6% non followers, so both reach cold, satire slightly wider. But comment rate splits hard: 0.6% and above baseline for Jimmy, 0.0% and below for the cookie dough.

The reason is Jimmy. A named character gives people something to quote, tag a mate under, and argue with. Satire is complete in itself, you get it and you swipe, there's nothing to add.

**The fix for satire: give them the thing to type.** End on a line that opens a door rather than closing one, so instead of stopping at "never once had a container of broccoli in my fridge", follow with something like "tell me the dumbest rule you've been given, I'll go first." Same joke, now with a reason to comment.

## 4. Length is not the problem

57 seconds held roughly 20% to the end on a 93.6% cold audience. 39 seconds held about the same. Stop assuming shorter is safer.

# THE TWO TEMPLATES

**Accusation**
```
Guess their situation with a specific number
Reverse it, same words, by second three
Buy time in four words
Signpost one metaphor, extend it, bridge back
Name the unfairness they have felt and never heard said
Give the comparison person a name and bring them back twice
Imperative close plus one swear on one absurd concrete image
```
Use for any myth where the effort someone is putting in is the actual cause of their problem. Long dieting, too much cardio, over restriction, training every day, obsessive tracking.

**Satire**
```
On screen text reveals the joke, audio never does
Over commit to the earnest setup
Escalate the absurdity, flatten the delivery each rung
Turn in four words, no explanation
Personal proof, not argument
Comment door on the very last line
```
Use for any rigid rule you can act out breaking while you recite it. Food rules, meal timing, prep culture, cardio rules, gym etiquette nonsense.
