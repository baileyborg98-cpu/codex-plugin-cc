Saw a comment the other day: "if you ate 1,000 calories a day of just donuts you'd get fat."

No you wouldn't. And it's not close.

Take a lifter who burns 2,500 calories a day. If he eats 2,000 calories of Skittles, Cinnamon Toast Crunch and chocolate milk — he loses weight. If he eats 3,000 calories of chicken and rice — he gains weight.

You can eat the healthiest food on earth. In a surplus, you gain. You can eat the worst food on earth. In a deficit, you lose. For the scale, it's calories in vs calories out. That's it.

But here's the part everyone skips.

Calories decide the number on the scale. Your macros decide what you're actually made of.

In a deficit on 2,000 calories of Reese's, you'll lose weight — and a load of muscle with it. In a deficit on 2,000 calories of chicken, beef and eggs, you'll lose weight and hold on to what you built. Same calories. Same weight lost. Completely different body.

Same goes for bulking. Surplus on candy and cereal and you'll pack on fat you didn't need. Surplus on real food and you'll add actual size — with some fat, because that's what a surplus is.

Count calories for the scale. Eat well for the physique.

They're not competing. They're answering two different questions.
