## The core idea

Fat loss and muscle growth are not opposites. The mechanisms are separate, so both run at once.

You can gain muscle and lose fat at the same time, at maintenance. No surplus needed unless you're already at single digit body fat. Over roughly 18% body fat for women, 10% for men, the body draws all the energy it needs for growth from the fat already on you.

**Maintenance is recomp.** The food you eat covers your energy expenditure, and your body uses the fat it's carrying to build the new muscle.

---

## The deficit fallacy

Too long in a deficit and the body goes into power saving mode. Move less, burn less, more tired, hungrier, worse workouts, hormones start misbehaving.

The plateau everyone hits is this: the body adapted, so the deficit stopped being a deficit. **Your old deficit quietly became your new maintenance.** That's why the weight stopped.

Long deficits lose weight early then stall. Short ones protect hormones and let you hold and even build muscle while losing fat.

## Maintenance

**It's a range, not a number.**

Do not trust online calculators. One client's calculator said 1700. Her true maintenance was 3000 to 3500, and she worked her way up to it.

Starting points for women, add 300 to 500 for men:

| Bodyweight | Start |
|---|---|
| 50 to 59kg | 2,000 |
| 60 to 69kg | 2,100 |
| 70 to 79kg | 2,200 |
| 80 to 89kg | 2,300 |
| 90 to 99kg | 2,400 |
| 100kg+ | 2,500 |

**When you hit true maintenance your weight jumps 1 to 2kg. That is glycogen refilling your muscles. It is not fat.** If it doesn't jump, you haven't eaten enough yet.

Method: hold for one to two weeks and watch the trend. Trending down, add 100. Trending up, drop 100 to 200. That's true maintenance.

The spike lands three to four days in. Use the weight from then on for your averages.

Retest every three to four months, or two to three times a year, because it moves.

## Mini cut protocol

Five week deficit, aiming for 1% of bodyweight per week.

One to two weeks back at maintenance to reset the metabolism. You don't gain fat in that window, you hold.

Still fat to lose, go again.

The reset is what makes the next deficit an actual deficit.

Find true maintenance first. Always.

## Recovery debt

This is the one nobody knows.

Training causes damage. Muscle protein synthesis stays elevated for only 48 to 72 hours after a session.

The body's first priority is getting back to baseline. Only once it's recovered does it use what's left over to build anything new. **Train too hard and every resource goes on repair, with nothing left for growth.** In a deficit that's where muscle loss and plateaus come from.

So: more isn't better. Better is better. Get the most growth out of a session while causing the least damage.

---

## Build training system

- **Frequency:** every muscle 2 to 3 times a week
- **Volume:** 2 to 6 working sets per muscle per session. 48 hours between sessions means 2 to 3 sets, 72 hours means 3 to 6
- **Reps:** 4 to 10 works, 5 to 8 is ideal
- **Rest:** minimum 2 minutes

**Never train a muscle once a week.** MPS drops off after 48 hours and atrophy starts, so on a once a week split you spend more of the week shrinking that muscle than growing it.

**Good exercises** load the muscle in the lengthened position, are stable enough to load properly, and have the target muscle as the limiting factor.

**Why low reps.** The more reps you do, the more things end the set before the muscle is anywhere near failure: the burn, the pain, being out of breath, the pump. New muscle means new contractile proteins, which means a stronger muscle. So getting stronger is proof you already built it. To grow fast, get strong, and high reps make that harder.

Rep ranges do not differ by muscle. No muscle "likes" high reps.

**Structure:** train the area you most want to change first, while you're fresh. Fresh means more stimulus and less fatigue in the way. Anything trained late gets less. If you want it to grow, train it. If you don't, train it less.

Warm up sets shouldn't build fatigue. A post activation potentiation set is one rep at working weight, then two minutes rest.

## Nutrient timing

Put 70% of your daily carbs around the workout.

- Morning training, 70% before lunch
- Afternoon or evening training, 70% after lunch

Same total carbs, better performance, more muscle for the same calories, and the body runs on fat for the rest of the day.

The body doesn't care what kind of carb it is. Complex is slower, simple is faster, that's the whole difference.

---

## Macros

- **Protein:** 1g per lb of bodyweight, or 2g per kg
- **Fat:** 0.5g per lb of bodyweight
- **Carbs:** whatever's left

**When you raise calories, raise them with carbs.** Keep protein and fat where they are.

## Perimenopause

**Your metabolism is not broken. A deficit still works. Energy balance doesn't stop being physics because your hormones are dysregulated.**

What actually changes:

- **Estrogen** declines and swings unpredictably. It helps you burn fat efficiently, keeps you insulin sensitive, and suppresses fat accumulation. When it drops, all three get harder, and fat starts going to the midsection.
- **Progesterone** drops hard because ovulation goes irregular. Estrogen stays dominant over the depleted progesterone, and that imbalance causes water retention and bloating that hides real fat loss on the scale.
- **Testosterone** declines too. More midsection fat, less energy.

After 40 it's genuinely harder to create and hold a deficit, because the hormonal environment changed. The strategies that worked at 35 are much less effective at 45.

**For muscle:** falling estrogen lowers MPS, slows repair, increases soreness and means you need longer rest. That's physiology, not a lack of effort. Insulin resistance rises. Lean mass drops and abdominal fat rises, which is why it feels like doing the same things for worse results.

**What works:** strength training, which raises MPS, improves insulin sensitivity, supports metabolism and protects bone density. Protein needs go up, so at least 2g per kg spread evenly across meals. Recovery becomes a priority rather than a weakness.

Building muscle in peri also reduces hot flashes, improves mood, improves sleep and reduces joint pain.
