The reason people get this backwards is not the science. It is that protein feels like effort and carbs feel like a reward.

So protein becomes the number you are proud of and carbs become the number you are quietly trying to keep down. Then you wonder why the last two sets of every session feel flat and why the load has not moved in five weeks.

Here is what that actually costs you. An 80kg lifter on three grams per kilo is eating 240 grams of protein. Come back to two and you free up around 320 calories. That is 80 grams of carbs a day you were not using for anything.

Eighty grams is the difference between finishing a session and surviving one.

And if you are in a deficit the order does not change. Protein stays, fats stay, and the carbs come down last, because carbs are the thing holding your training quality together while the calories drop. Cut them first and you will hold on to less muscle, not more.

Protein is the material. Training is the trigger. Carbs are what let you pull the trigger hard enough to matter.
