# Travel Day

Written on the move. Three scripts for the Leeds to Brisbane run, plus the shot list, because the footage is the bit you can't go back for.

---

## THE BIG ONE: 32 hours

**Format:** Sequence. Two second beats, flat factual delivery, meaning saved for the last line.

**On screen:** 32 hours

```
0:00  Thirty two hours from here to Brisbane.
0:02  Doncaster platform.
0:04  Train down to London.
0:06  Tube across the city with a bag I packed badly.
0:09  Heathrow. Bag drop. The long walk.
0:12  Thirteen hours to Singapore.
0:14  Two hours in a terminal at four in the morning.
0:17  Coffee I absolutely do not need.
0:19  Eight more hours.
0:21  Land in the city I grew up in.
```

**Land:** Thirty two hours to where I'm from. Home's the bit I just left.

**Alternate land:** Funny thing is I'm not going home. I'm leaving it.

**Delivery:** No talking head anywhere. Every beat is a real shot, cut on the beat. Keep the voiceover completely flat, no warmth, no selling, because the last line does all the work and it only lands if nothing before it reached for anything. Record the voiceover at the end of the journey when you sound wrecked, not now.

**Why this one matters.** It's the Corfu structure, which is your best performing format, and the reframe at the end is better than Corfu's because it's about you rather than about annual leave. The line is yours too, you said it out loud without thinking about it, which is usually the sign.

## FILM NOW: the platform one

**Format:** Observation. You can shoot this in the next ten minutes.

**On screen:** nobody has ever stood in the right place on a platform

```
0:00  Nobody has ever stood in the right place on a platform.
0:03  You pick a spot.
0:05  You commit to it.
0:07  You've got a feeling about this one.
0:09  Train pulls in, and the doors stop four metres away
0:12  from you and the nine other people who agreed with you.
0:15  So everybody does the shuffle.
0:17  Not a run. You're not an animal.
0:20  Just the fast walk that says you meant to do that.
```

**Land:** Every station. Every time. Twenty eight years of it.

**Delivery:** Handheld, phone low, film the actual platform and the actual people. Say it under your breath like you're narrating it to someone stood next to you. If a train pulls in mid take, keep rolling and film the shuffle, that's the whole video.

## FOR THE LAYOVER: the wrecked one

**Format:** Pub yap. Film it in Singapore at four in the morning when you look and sound like death, because that's the entire point.

**On screen:** hour 19

```
0:00  Right, hour nineteen.
0:03  I'm in Singapore, it's four in the morning,
0:06  and I've got no idea what day it is for anybody.
0:09  And I do this run once or twice a year now,
0:12  and every single time, somewhere around here,
0:15  I get the same thought.
0:17  This is the price. This exact bit.
0:20  Not the visa, not the money, not any of the stuff people ask about.
0:24  It's this. Thirty two hours, twice a year, forever.
0:28  And I'd still do it.
```

**Land:** Everything good I've got has a version of this attached to it.

**Delivery:** One shot, phone propped on a bag, no lighting, no tidying yourself up. Do not film this fresh at the start of the journey, it only works if you genuinely look like you've been awake for nineteen hours.

## SHOT LIST

Get these as you go. Five seconds each is plenty and you won't get another run at them.

- Doncaster platform, wide, the board above you
- The train pulling in
- Out the window, moving
- Kings Cross or wherever you come out, the crowd
- Tube doors closing
- Heathrow bag drop
- The walk to the gate, from behind if someone's with you, otherwise your feet
- Plane window on the runway
- Cabin lights dimmed, mid flight
- Singapore terminal, empty, four in the morning
- The coffee
- Second boarding
- Window on descent into Brisbane
- First step outside, the heat hitting

That's fourteen shots. Thirteen of them are the 32 hours reel and the last two are worth having regardless.

---

## One note

Don't film the big one's voiceover until you land. The script above is what you say, but you want to sound like someone at the end of thirty two hours, not someone at the start of it. The line about home is not a line you can perform fresh.
