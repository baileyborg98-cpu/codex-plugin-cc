You finished the cut. Now here is where most people quietly undo it.

They reverse diet. Fifty calories a week. Slow and careful, because going straight back up feels reckless after all that work.

I understand the logic. It is still the wrong move.

Because a reverse diet is not a gentle exit from a deficit. It is a longer stay in one. Twelve weeks of adding fifty calories at a time is twelve more weeks with your hormones flat, your metabolic rate suppressed, and your body still short on energy. And a body short on energy keeps taking the difference out of your muscle.

That is why people come out of a long reverse looking softer than they did at the end of the cut. Not because they gained fat. Because they lost the thing that was giving them shape, and they did it slowly enough not to notice.

Going straight back to maintenance is the faster fix and the harder one mentally, because the scale moves immediately. Expect roughly 2% of your body weight in the first week or so.

That number is not fat. You cannot eat enough in seven days to gain that much. It is glycogen refilling, water coming back with it, and more food physically sitting in you. It is your body switching back on.

And once it is back on, it actually does something with the food. Hormones refill. Metabolic rate climbs. And you hold on to the fat you worked to lose instead of slowly leaking muscle for another three months.

So cut for five weeks. Take the break properly, at maintenance, not at maintenance minus two hundred. Then go again.

The break is not the end of your fat loss. It is what lets it continue.
