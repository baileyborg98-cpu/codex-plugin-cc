# EDUCATION

---

## 1. The calculator

**On screen:** her calculator said 1700

```
0:00  Client came to me eating fourteen hundred calories a day.
0:04  Her online calculator had told her maintenance was seventeen hundred,
0:08  so she'd spent two years living just under that,
0:11  permanently hungry, permanently stuck.
0:14  Took us about four months to feed her back up.
0:17  Her actual maintenance was over three thousand.
0:20  Three thousand.
0:22  She'd been walking around on half of what her body could handle,
0:25  calling herself lazy for not losing weight.
```

**Land:** A website made that number up. She built two years around it.

**Delivery:** Say "three thousand" on its own and let it sit. Everything after it goes quieter, not louder.

## 2. The plateau

**On screen:** why the weight stopped

```
0:00  Everyone thinks a plateau means they've gone soft,
0:03  so they cut more.
0:05  Here's what's actually happened.
0:07  You've been in a deficit long enough that the body adapted.
0:11  Moves less. Burns less. Runs the whole operation on less.
0:15  So the number you were losing weight on
0:17  has quietly turned into the number you maintain on.
0:21  Your deficit became your maintenance
0:23  and nobody sent you a letter about it.
```

**Land:** Eat more for a fortnight. Then it'll move again.

**Delivery:** Flat and matter of fact throughout. The letter line is the only smile in it.

## 3. The kilo

**On screen:** the scale went up and that's the good news

```
0:00  When I put someone up to their real maintenance
0:03  the scale goes up a kilo or two.
0:06  Every single time.
0:08  And every single time, they panic.
0:11  That weight is glycogen.
0:13  It's your muscles filling back up with fuel and water
0:16  after years of running on empty.
0:19  Arrives in about three days, then it sits still.
0:22  And if it never happens, you still haven't eaten enough.
```

**Land:** That kilo is the whole point.

**Delivery:** Film next to a scale but don't stand on it. Calm, almost reassuring. This one's for the person who's frightened.

## 4. Once a week

**On screen:** chest day. once a week.

```
0:00  If you're training a muscle once a week,
0:03  here's what happens in between.
0:05  Muscle protein synthesis runs for about forty eight hours
0:08  after you train it.
0:10  Then it stops.
0:12  And the muscle starts going the other way.
0:15  So on a once a week split
0:17  you get two days of building
0:19  and five days of slowly giving it back.
```

**Land:** You're shrinking it more days than you're growing it. Hit it twice.

**Delivery:** Gym, whiteboard or your hand counting days out. The five days line is the one that lands, so slow down there.

## 5. Recovery debt

**On screen:** more isn't better

```
0:00  I used to think a session didn't count unless it destroyed me.
0:04  Two hours. Everything to failure. Barely walking out.
0:08  Here's the bit I didn't know.
0:10  Your body's first job is getting back to where it started.
0:14  Only what's left after that goes on building anything new.
0:18  So I was spending every resource I had
0:20  repairing damage I'd caused on purpose,
0:23  and wondering why nothing ever grew.
```

**Land:** More isn't better. Better is better.

**Delivery:** Gym car park after a session, not during. Slightly rueful, you're taking the piss out of your younger self.

## 6. Your metabolism isn't broken

**On screen:** your metabolism is not broken

```
0:00  If you're in your forties and everything that used to work
0:03  has stopped working,
0:05  hear me on one thing.
0:07  Your metabolism is not broken.
0:10  Energy balance doesn't stop being physics
0:12  because your hormones are all over the place.
0:15  What changed is the environment.
0:17  Estrogen's dropping, so you're less insulin sensitive,
0:20  recovery's slower,
0:22  and the scale's holding water that's hiding fat
0:24  you have genuinely already lost.
0:27  It's harder now. It was never impossible.
```

**Land:** It was never a lack of effort. It's physiology.

**Delivery:** Straight to camera, sat down, no gym in shot. This is the most important one in the batch and it should look like the least produced.

## 7. Leaning down the mountain

**On screen:** the only way to turn

```
0:00  The thing nobody tells you about learning to snowboard
0:03  is that to turn you have to lean down the mountain.
0:07  Down it.
0:09  Towards the drop.
0:11  And every instinct you have says lean back into the hill
0:14  where it feels safe,
0:16  and leaning back is the exact thing that puts you on your arse.
0:20  Took me two days to make myself do it.
0:23  Second I did, I could ride.
```

**Land:** Lean at the thing you're scared of. It's the only bit that works.

**Delivery:** Snowboard footage over the top, no talking head. If you've got a clip of yourself falling, open on that.

**Delivery:** Archive footage, the worse the better. Do not use a good clip of yourself anywhere in this.

```
0:00  People think this job is writing training programmes.
0:04  That's about four percent of it.
0:06  The rest is talking someone out of quitting on a Wednesday.
```

**Land:** You're not paying me for the spreadsheet.

**Delivery:** At the desk, laptop open, mid work day. No gym, no gear. The point is that none of it looks like fitness.

## Batch notes

Numbers 1 to 6 all come out of the masterclass notes, so every claim in them is yours and can be said without hedging.

Number 6 is the highest value script in anything I've written for you. It speaks to the exact person who buys coaching and it tells her she isn't the problem.

Numbers 7 and 8 are the first snowboarding ones. I've kept them to things that are true of learning to ride rather than inventing trips or places, so swap in your real details where you've got them.

Number 9 is a sister to the Delilah script in the big batch. Pick one, don't run both.
