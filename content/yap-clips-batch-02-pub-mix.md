# Yap Clip Batch 02 — The Pub Mix

25 scripts. 15 genuine pub yaps (life, everyday thoughts, the stuff everyone thinks but nobody films) + 10 value yaps (who you coach, where you specialise). All in your voice: raw, direct, punchy sentences mixed with longer philosophical ones, metaphor-heavy, swears where it's natural, endings that hit.

Sourced from your real life, not templates: the wedding, the Sahara, the guilt tax call, the 80/20 rule, above/beneath the surface goals, the dead hang challenge, crumpets and honey, "squat low, standards high".

**Timing:** 30s ≈ 75–80 words · 45s ≈ 110–115 · 60s ≈ 150–155. Read once out loud before filming; you talk quick, so most will land a few seconds under. Perfect.

## PUB YAPS

### 1. The snooze button — 30s
**Hook:** The snooze button is the first negotiation you lose every day.

Think about it. You, fully asleep, made a deal with you, fully awake. And the sleepy version — the one with zero information and zero plans — overrules the deal in one slap.

Nine more minutes. Then nine more.

Nobody in the history of the world has felt better after the third snooze.

You don't need more sleep at that point, legend. You need a reason to get up that's louder than the duvet.

*Delivery: groggy voice on "nine more minutes". Grin at the end.*

---

### 2. Airports — 45s
**Hook:** Airports are proof that humans have lost the plot.

I've been in a lot of airports lately, and I watch this every time. Gate opens. Everyone leaps up like the plane's leaving without them. Sprint. Elbows. Panic.

Then they stand in a queue for twenty minutes… to sit in a seat… that has their name on it… that is going nowhere without them.

We rush to wait. That's the whole thing.

And then I thought — mate, that's not just airports. That's half of life. Rushing to a queue. Panicking to get somewhere that was always going to be there.

The seat's booked, legend. Walk.

*Delivery: act out the leap. Long pause before "The seat's booked."*

---

### 3. British weather — 30s
**Hook:** This country has two personalities and both of them are complaining.

Nine months of the year: "it's grim, it's raining, I'm moving to Spain."

Then we get one week at twenty-six degrees and the whole nation collapses. News warnings. "Stay indoors." Blokes going grey buying paddling pools.

Twenty-six degrees! That's a mild Tuesday in half the world.

We don't want good weather. We want something to talk about at the kettle. And honestly? Fair enough.

*Delivery: full pub-rant energy. Affectionate, not bitter.*

---

### 4. The Sahara — 60s
**Hook:** I slept in the Sahara Desert and my brain finally shut up.

On the honeymoon we spent a night in the Sahara. No signal. None. Phone was a brick with a camera.

And I'm lying there looking at more stars than I knew existed, and I noticed something mad — the noise stopped. Not the desert. The desert's silent. The noise in my head. The tabs. The replay of conversations. The list of stuff I should be doing.

Gone. First time in years.

And here's the uncomfortable bit — the desert didn't do that. The missing signal did. Which means the noise isn't life. The noise is the phone. I carry the noise in my pocket, voluntarily, and I top up the battery so it never has to stop.

You don't need a desert, legend. You need ten minutes a day where the thing in your pocket can't reach you.

I'm working on it too. But those stars, man.

*Delivery: story pace, quieter than usual. Let "the noise stopped" breathe.*

### 5. The wedding — 45s
**Hook:** I just got married, and the big moment wasn't the big moment.

Everyone tells you about the big bits. The aisle. The speeches. The first dance. And they were class, don't get me wrong.

But the bit that actually got me? A quiet thirty seconds off to the side, just the two of us, mid-chaos, when she looked at me like — we did it. Nobody photographed it. It won't be in the album.

And that's the lesson I keep re-learning about everything. The album moments are for other people. The real ones are small, quiet, and nearly missed.

Life's not the highlight reel, legend. It's the outtakes. Guard them.

*Delivery: soft. This one's vulnerable — don't perform it, just say it.*

### 6. The group chat holiday — 30s
**Hook:** Every group chat has a holiday that's been "getting planned" for three years.

You know the one. "Boys, this summer, we're doing it." Seventy messages. Screenshots of villas. Someone makes a poll.

Nothing. Ever. Happens.

Because a plan without a booking is just a chat. Someone has to buy the flights and let the rest catch up.

That's true for the holiday and it's true for your whole life, legend. Stop planning in the group chat. Book the flight.

*Delivery: everyone's lived this — big grin, point at the camera on the last line.*

### 7. The trolley wheel — 30s
**Hook:** I fought a shopping trolley for forty-five minutes yesterday and it taught me something.

Grabbed the one with the dodgy wheel. You know instantly. First push — brrrrrr — pulling left.

And did I walk ten metres back and swap it? Course not. I wrestled that thing round the entire shop like an idiot.

How much of life is that? Something's clearly not working, the fix is ten metres away, and we just… lean harder.

Swap the trolley, legend.

*Delivery: act out the wonky push. Deadpan on the last line.*

### 8. Nobody's watching you at the gym — 45s
**Hook:** Everyone at the gym is scared of everyone at the gym. Nobody at the gym is looking at anyone at the gym.

I hear this constantly from people starting out — "I feel judged, everyone's watching me."

Legend. Listen. That bloke by the dumbbells? He's rehearsing his own set in his head and worrying about his hairline. The woman on the squat rack? She's counting reps and thinking about an email. The lad in the hoodie? He's been planning his exit past the mirrors so nobody sees HIM.

It's a room full of people worried about being judged by people worried about being judged. Nobody's got a spare thought for you. That's not sad — that's freedom.

The audience you're scared of doesn't exist. Train.

*Delivery: point around an imaginary gym for each character.*

### 9. Tired vs drained — 45s
**Hook:** Being tired and being drained are not the same thing, and mixing them up will ruin your life.

Some days I do eight hours of client calls back to back and finish absolutely buzzing. Knackered — but full.

Other days I do one hour of some pointless admin nonsense and I need to lie down in a dark room.

Same energy spent. Completely different damage.

Tired comes from effort. Drained comes from spending yourself on stuff that means nothing to you. Sleep fixes tired. Sleep does not fix drained — only changing what you're doing fixes drained.

So before you book a holiday, legend, check which one you are. You might not need a week off. You might need a different week.

*Delivery: the last two lines are the clip. Slow right down.*

### 10. One more episode — 30s
**Hook:** "One more episode" is rarely about the show.

Be honest. Half the time you're not even enjoying it. You're watching someone else's story so you don't have to sit with yours.

The show ends, you feel worse, you press next anyway.

I'm not anti-Netflix, legend — rest is rest. But there's a difference between resting and hiding. And deep down, you always know which one you're doing.

*Delivery: gentle, no judgement. You've done it too — let that show.*

### 11. The fridge — 30s
**Hook:** Checking your phone when you're bored is opening the fridge when you're not hungry.

You know there's nothing new in there. You checked four minutes ago. Same milk. Same leftovers. Same three apps in a circle.

But you open it anyway, stand there, close it. And you'll do it again in ten minutes.

You're not looking for something, legend. You're avoiding something. The fridge never has the answer.

*Delivery: mime opening the fridge. Shrug at the end.*

### 12. The old boy at the gym — 45s
**Hook:** The fittest person at my gym is about seventy and he's never had a motivational day in his life.

Every gym's got him. In at the same time every morning. Same little routine. Vest older than me. Does his bits, nods at people, off he goes.

He's not tracking streaks. He's not posting it. He doesn't need a pre-workout and a playlist to get through the door. Showing up stopped being a decision for him about forty years ago — it's just who he is now.

That's the actual finish line, legend. Not the body. Not the numbers. The day it stops being effort and starts being identity.

Be the old boy.

*Delivery: warm. "Be the old boy" with total sincerity.*

### 13. I'll start Monday — 30s
**Hook:** Monday isn't a date. It's a hiding place.

"I'll start Monday" sounds like a plan, doesn't it? It's got a day attached. Feels official.

But you've said it before. On a Tuesday. Which means you chose to wait five days to avoid starting for four of them.

There's nothing magic about Monday, legend. The version of you that starts Monday is the same one available right now — just with a worse excuse.

It's today. It was always today.

*Delivery: cheeky open, dead serious final line.*

### 14. The dead hang — 45s
**Hook:** We started a dead hang challenge in our community and it's accidentally the most honest test of life I've seen.

Rules are simple. Grab the bar. Hang. That's it. No technique, no talent — just you versus the urge to let go.

And here's what's funny: everyone's fine for the first thirty seconds. Everyone. The first thirty seconds costs nothing.

It's the bit after — when the forearms scream and your brain starts making legal arguments about why you've done enough — that's where the entire game lives.

Sound familiar? Most people don't fail things, legend. They let go of things. There's a difference, and your hands know it before your head does.

Go hang off a bar. See who you are.

*Delivery: grip an imaginary bar. Strain face on "legal arguments".*

### 15. "You've changed" — 30s
**Hook:** Someone says "you've changed" like it's an insult. Mate — that was the whole point.

What's the alternative? "Congratulations, you're identical to 2019 you"? That's not loyalty, legend. That's fear with a routine.

You're supposed to change. Skin does it. Seasons do it. Every cell in your body's on rotation.

The people upset that you changed were comfortable with who you were. And comfortable for them usually meant small for you.

Change louder.

*Delivery: fire in this one. "Change louder" is a mic drop.*

## VALUE YAPS

### 16. The guilt tax — 45s
**Hook:** You're paying a tax you don't even know exists. I call it the guilt tax.

It's the energy you burn apologising for your own goals. Explaining why you're not drinking. Justifying leaving the party at ten. Softening your ambition so nobody at the table feels uncomfortable.

That's a tax, legend. And you're paying it out of the exact energy account you need to actually chase the goal.

Here's the move: stop filing the paperwork. "No thanks, I've got training in the morning" is a complete sentence. No appendix. No apology.

The people who matter don't need the explanation. The people who need the explanation don't matter to the goal.

Keep your energy. Spend it on the chase.

*Delivery: this is a hill you actually die on — let it show.*

---

### 17. Above and beneath the surface — 60s
**Hook:** The goal you tell people is never the goal that keeps you going.

Every goal has two layers, legend.

Above the surface: the visible stuff. Lose the weight. See the abs. Fit the jeans. That's the goal you'd say out loud at a barbecue.

Beneath the surface: the real stuff. Proving you're not someone who quits. Self-respect. Discipline. Being the parent your kids catch being consistent, not the one they catch giving up.

Now here's why this matters. Above-the-surface goals are weather. The scale has a bad week, the mirror has a bad day, and suddenly the whole thing feels pointless.

Beneath-the-surface goals don't care about the weather. You can honour "I keep my word to myself" on your worst day. In fact your worst day is the only day it really counts.

So dig, legend. Ask "why" until it gets uncomfortable. Whatever's down there — that's your engine. The abs are just the exhaust.

*Delivery: hands — high for surface, low for beneath. "The abs are just the exhaust" earns a beat.*

### 18. 80/20 — 30s
**Hook:** One hundred percent dedication is how you end up face-down in a bakery.

I tell my clients: eighty percent dedication, twenty percent living. On purpose.

The pizza with your kids. The wine at the wedding. Scheduled. Guilt-free. Part of the plan, not a crack in it.

Because the all-or-nothing crowd always ends up at nothing, legend. Every time.

Perfect for six weeks or consistent for six years. Pick one. Only one of them changes your life.

*Delivery: punchy. Count 80/20 on your fingers.*

---

### 19. When life falls apart, shrink the plan — 45s
**Hook:** When your life goes sideways, don't quit the plan. Shrink it.

Had a client recently going through absolute chaos — real life stuff, the heavy kind. And her instinct was the one everyone has: "I can't do all this right now, so I'll stop everything."

No, legend. That's the all-or-nothing trap wearing a sad costume.

We didn't stop. We shrank. Forget tracking ten things — hit your protein and get a walk in. That's it. That's the whole plan until the storm passes.

Because the goal in a crisis isn't progress. It's keeping the thread. A tiny plan you keep beats a perfect plan you abandoned — and when life calms down, you're still in the building.

Shrink it. Don't drop it.

*Delivery: compassionate open, firm middle. This is your actual coaching philosophy — own it.*

### 20. Your kids are taking notes — 45s
**Hook:** Your kids will never remember your macros. They'll remember that you kept showing up.

Parents tell me they feel selfish taking an hour to train. Selfish! Legend, flip it.

Your kids don't listen to a word you say — you know this, you've asked them to tidy their room. They copy what you DO. They're little CCTV cameras with your DNA.

So what's on the tape? Someone who quit every January? Or someone who trained tired, ate well, kept promises to themselves, and never made a big song and dance about it?

You're not taking an hour from them. You're recording a lesson they'll play for the rest of their lives: this is what looking after yourself looks like.

Train. They're watching. Good.

*Delivery: "little CCTV cameras" gets the laugh, the ending gets the share.*

### 21. Gratitude and ambition — 45s
**Hook:** "Just be grateful for what you have" — okay, but have you considered doing both?

There's this idea floating about that gratitude and ambition are enemies. That wanting more means you don't appreciate what you've got.

Rubbish, legend. They're not opposites. They're your two eyes.

Gratitude looks back — look how far we've come. Ambition looks forward — look how far we can go. Close either one and you lose all depth perception.

Grateful with no ambition, you settle. Ambitious with no gratitude, you're miserable at every milestone, moving the goalposts before the ball's even landed.

The move is the middle: celebrate the win properly, then want the next one. Both eyes open.

That's not greed. That's vision.

*Delivery: cover one eye, then the other. "That's vision" — slight smile.*

### 22. Twenty years in, she started again — 60s
**Hook:** A client of mine just quit a twenty-year career at forty-five. Best decision she's made.

Twenty years in the same industry. Good at it. Respected. And quietly, completely burnt out — the kind of burnout where Sunday night starts on Saturday morning.

Through her transformation she found something she actually loved, and last month she handed in her notice to go do it full time. Twenty years, gone. New chapter.

Now — some people will call that a waste. "All that experience!"

Wrong way round, legend. The twenty years weren't wasted. They were the tuition. Every skill, every hard conversation, every early morning — it all comes with her.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: you're allowed to outgrow the thing you built. The cost of a fresh start is real. But the cost of staying somewhere your soul already left? That compounds daily.

Forty-five isn't too late. It's half-time.

*Delivery: story mode. "Sunday night starts on Saturday morning" — everyone who's had that job will feel it.*

### 23. Crumpets and honey — 30s
**Hook:** I put crumpets and honey in a client's nutrition plan and she nearly fell over.

"I'm allowed this?" Allowed. Like food's a parole officer.

Legend, listen. If your plan only works when you eat like a monk, it doesn't work. Full stop. You'll white-knuckle it for eight weeks and then eat the whole bakery on week nine.

The best plan isn't the strictest one. It's the one you can still be doing at Christmas. And ours has crumpets in it.

*Delivery: her voice on "I'm allowed this?". Proud grin on the last line.*

### 24. Punch today in the face — 30s
**Hook:** A client gave me a mindset I'm never giving back: punch today in the face.

Some days you wake up flat. No fire, no vision-board energy, motivation's left the building. The gurus have nothing for you.

Those days, you don't need inspiration. You need aggression with a to-do list.

Don't feel your way into the day. Swing first. Coffee, boots, first task, GO — mood catches up later, it always does.

Today's got a jaw, legend. You know what to do.

*Delivery: full energy, half-laughing. Fist on the last line.*

### 25. What I'd tell the younger me — 60s
**Hook:** If I could have one pint with the younger version of me, I know exactly what I'd say.

And it's not "buy Bitcoin", although — mate.

I'd tell him the failures he's so embarrassed about are the tuition fees. Every plan that fell apart, every time he started again in the dark thinking he was behind everyone — none of it was wasted. It was the curriculum.

I'd tell him to stop performing confidence and start building evidence. Confidence isn't a mood, it's a receipt collection — kept promises, hard mornings, boring reps nobody clapped for.

And I'd tell him to relax about the timeline. The stuff you're panicking about at twenty-five doesn't even make the list at the wedding, in the desert, on the good days that are coming.

Then I'd finish the pint and say the annoying bit: you'll ignore all of this, because the ignoring is part of the curriculum too.

Cheers, legend. See you up ahead.

*Delivery: this is the campfire clip. Slow, warm, raise an imaginary glass at the end.*

## Batch Notes

- **Mix:** 15 pub yaps (1–15), 10 value yaps (16–25). Runtime spread: 11 × 30s, 10 × 45s, 4 × 60s.
- **Posting rhythm:** alternate — pub yap, value yap, pub yap. The pub ones earn the trust; the value ones spend it. Never post three value clips in a row.
- **The stories are yours:** Sahara, wedding, dead hang, crumpets, the career-change client, the guilt tax — all from your real life and calls. Say them like memories, not scripts. If a detail's slightly off, correct it on camera — that's more you than reading it right.
- **Swearing:** kept it natural and light. Turn it up or down on delivery, you know your platforms.
- **Want the other 25?** This is the 25 batch. Happy to build a second 25 (fresh pub topics — driving, sleep, money talk at the barbecue, holiday Bailey vs home Bailey) to make it the full 50.
- **Final polish tip:** run any of these through your `baileys-dna-voice` skill in a claude.ai chat before filming — that skill has your master voice reference and this session could only see its description, not its full contents.
