# Bailey Borg — Voice DNA + Yap Clip Batch 01

## Part 1: Who You Are (Voice DNA)

Built from your sent emails, your Retention Mastery deck, and your team call notes.

**Identity:** Bailey Borg, Head of Client Success at Verto Coaching. You're the long-game guy — the one who keeps people in the journey after the launch hype fades. Your lane is retention, connection, and making everyday people (mostly busy women juggling real life) feel seen while they transform.

**Core beliefs (straight from your own material):**
- Retention is structure, not luck. Staying should be the natural outcome, not a hope.
- Relying on motivation is a strategy for failure.
- The scale is just one data point — energy, sleep, confidence and consistency are the real transformation.
- Clients don't leave because of slow results. They leave when communication fades and they stop feeling part of the process.
- Retention doesn't start when someone thinks about leaving. It starts the moment they join.
- Proactive beats reactive: "We don't wait for them to reach out; we lead the way."
- Future casting: always show people what's next, so the journey never has a cliff edge.

**How you actually talk (from your real emails):**
- "Hey legend" / "legend" is your signature address — warm, equalising, never patronising.
- Short, punchy sentences. Zero corporate fluff. You get to the point in one or two lines.
- Warm-blunt: "Should be back on the app legend." "Let me look into this legend and get a message back to you."
- Protective and human: you cancelled a client's midnight call and told her to get some rest — "we won't be doing any calls past 8-9pm at night let alone midnight!!"
- Double exclamation marks when you mean it. "Good stuff." "No worries at all." "Cheers" to sign off.
- UK English. Speaks like a mate who happens to be elite at his job.

**Voice rules for any script in your voice:**
1. Open like you're mid-conversation, not presenting.
2. One idea per clip. Say it, prove it, land it.
3. Talk TO one person ("you"), never AT an audience.
4. Use "legend" once, naturally — usually the open or the close, never both.
5. Warm-blunt truth over hype. No "crushing it" influencer speak.
6. End with a line they'll repeat to someone else.

## Part 2: Yap Clip Batch 01

Nine clips: three at 30s, three at 45s, three at 60s.
Pacing assumption: ~150–160 words per minute conversational. 30s ≈ 75 words, 45s ≈ 115 words, 60s ≈ 155 words.
No B-roll needed — these are pure talking-to-camera yaps. Hook is the first line on screen and out of your mouth.

---

### 30-SECOND CLIPS

#### Clip 1 — "The scale is one data point" (~76 words)
**Hook:** The scale is lying to you. Sort of.

Hey legend, quick one. The scale is one data point. That's it. One.

You slept better this week. Your energy's up. You handled a stressful Tuesday without inhaling the biscuit tin. Your jeans fit different.

That's five data points against one.

So why are you letting the one with the worst attitude make all the decisions?

Weigh the whole week, not just yourself.

**Delivery:** Calm open, cheeky on "worst attitude", slow down for the last line.

---

#### Clip 2 — "The motivation fallacy" (~77 words)
**Hook:** Motivation was never going to save you.

Relying on motivation is a strategy for failure. I say that with love, legend.

Motivation got you started. Brilliant. It was always going to leave. It leaves everyone — me included.

What stays is structure. The check-in you don't skip. The workout that's booked like a meeting. The plan that doesn't care about your mood.

Motivated people start transformations.

Structured people finish them.

Build the structure while the motivation's still in the room.

**Delivery:** "I say that with love" is the smile moment. Hit "start" vs "finish" hard.

---

#### Clip 3 — "The Week 3 wall" (~78 words)
**Hook:** Week 3 is where most transformations quietly die.

Nobody quits in week one. Week one is exciting — new plan, new you, everyone's buzzing.

Week 3 is where it gets real. The novelty's gone, the scale's being moody, and life starts asking for its time back.

Here's the thing, legend — week 3 isn't a sign it's not working. It's a sign it's working exactly like it always does.

Everyone hits the wall. The ones who transform just don't turn around at it.

**Delivery:** Conspiratorial tone on "quietly die". Reassuring, not preachy, at the end.

### 45-SECOND CLIPS

#### Clip 4 — "The gym membership principle" (~114 words)
**Hook:** Why do you cancel one membership and keep the other?

Think about two gyms, legend.

Gym A emails you tips, checks your progress, celebrates your milestones, runs little challenges. Gym B charges your card every month and says absolutely nothing.

When money gets tight, which one goes first? Gym B. Every time. Not because the equipment's worse — because you feel nothing for it.

Connection creates commitment. That's true for gyms, and it's true for every habit in your life.

So if your health routine feels like Gym B — transactional, silent, joyless — don't ask why you keep quitting it. Make it something you're attached to. Train with people. Track your wins. Celebrate them out loud.

You don't quit things you feel part of.

**Delivery:** Set up the two gyms with your hands, left and right. Pause after "Every time."

---

#### Clip 5 — "Retention starts on day one" (for the coaches) (~113 words)
**Hook:** Your client decided whether they're renewing in their first week.

Coaches, listen. Retention doesn't start when a client thinks about leaving. It starts the moment they join.

Most coaches crush the front end — welcome video, big energy, hype for days. Then week 4 arrives and it's tumbleweed. Robotic check-ins. Copy-paste replies.

And here's the truth nobody wants to hear: clients don't leave because of slow results. They leave when communication fades and they stop feeling part of the process.

So build the structure. Week 1: connection. Week 3: catch the struggles. Mid-program: show them how far they've come. Final quarter: plant the seeds for what's next.

Don't wait for them to reach out, legend. Lead the way.

**Delivery:** Straight to camera, coach-to-coach energy. The "tumbleweed" line earns a beat.

---

#### Clip 6 — "Future casting" (~112 words)
**Hook:** The goal is not the finish line. There is no finish line.

Legend, can I tell you the biggest mistake I see people make? They aim at a goal like it's the end of the story.

Lose the weight. Hit the number. Done. Credits roll.

Then they get there — or close — and there's nothing on the other side. No vision. No next chapter. So they drift straight back to the old version of themselves.

The people who keep their results do one thing differently: before they finish one phase, they're already looking at the next one. New target. New skill. New reason to keep showing up.

Don't just set a goal. Set a direction.

Goals end. Directions don't.

**Delivery:** "Credits roll" gets a little smirk. Last two lines slow and certain.

### 60-SECOND CLIPS

#### Clip 7 — "The highlight message" (~152 words)
**Hook:** I sent a client one message and it changed her entire journey.

Let me tell you about a message I send, legend, because you might need to send it to yourself.

I was reviewing a client's check-ins from the last month. On paper? Scale had barely moved. She was gutted. Ready to call the whole thing a failure.

So I wrote back: you've hit 90% of your workouts. Your energy's up. You're sleeping through the night. You handled the most stressful month of your year without falling apart. The scale is one data point — THIS is the real transformation.

She read it back and cried. Not because it was clever — because it was true, and she couldn't see it.

Here's the thing: you are the worst judge of your own progress. You live inside it. You need a mirror.

So do this today. Look back at where you were eight weeks ago. Honestly. All of it.

You've come further than you think.

**Delivery:** Story mode — slower, personal. The list in the middle builds pace, then drop right down for "she cried".

---

#### Clip 8 — "Why they actually quit" (for the coaches) (~154 words)
**Hook:** Your clients aren't leaving because of results. I promise you.

Coaches — hard truth time, legend.

When a client leaves, everyone blames results. "She wasn't losing fast enough." No. I've watched clients with incredible results walk away, and clients with slow scales stay for years.

The difference is never the number. It's whether they still felt part of something.

Think about when you cancel anything — a gym, a subscription, an app. It's not the day it stops working. It's the day you realise nobody would notice if you left.

That's the moment your client quietly decides. Not at renewal. Weeks before, when the replies got robotic and the check-in became a form instead of a conversation.

Structure plus emotion. That's the whole game. Systematic touchpoints so nobody slips through, genuine connection so nobody feels like a number.

Ask yourself honestly: if your quietest client disappeared tomorrow, would your system catch it — or would you find out at renewal?

**Delivery:** Serious open, hands still. The final question is the whole clip — leave a beat of silence after it.

---

#### Clip 9 — "The long game" (~153 words)
**Hook:** Twelve weeks will change your body. The long game changes your life.

Legend, let's be honest about something.

Everyone wants the 12-week transformation. And look — 12 weeks is real. You can change a lot in 12 weeks. I've watched it hundreds of times.

But I've also watched what happens after. And the after is where the actual game is played.

Because the people who keep it? They stopped treating health like a project with a deadline and started treating it like a place they live. They found rhythms instead of chasing streaks. They celebrated boring wins — the packed lunch, the morning walk, the early night. They built a life their results could survive in.

The 12-week version of you is a preview. The five-year version is the film.

So yes — go hard for 12 weeks. Genuinely. But when someone asks how long your programme is, I want you thinking: the rest of my life, mate.

And meaning it.

**Delivery:** Big-brother energy. "A place they live" is the line — slow it down. Grin on the last two words.

## Batch Notes

- **Mix:** 7 client-facing, 2 coach-facing (Clips 5 and 8). If your feed is purely client-facing, hold those two back for a coach-education push.
- **Posting order suggestion:** 3 → 1 → 6 → 2 → 4 → 9 → 7 → 5 → 8 (start with the most relatable hook, end each week on a story or long-game clip).
- **Captions:** first line of the caption = the hook, then one sentence, then a soft CTA ("Send this to someone in week 3").
- **Timing check:** read each aloud once before filming; if you're naturally quicker than 155 wpm, the 60s scripts land around 55s, which is perfect — never rush to fill.
